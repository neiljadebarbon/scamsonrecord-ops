# The narrator of Scams on Record

Read this before writing any voiceover, caption or post. The channel is faceless. The narrator is a voice and nothing else.

## Who the narrator is
- A calm, plain-spoken voice that tells real scam cases from the public record and ends each one with what to do instead.
- The narrator never says who they are, never claims a job, a badge, a credential or a past. No "as a former investigator", no "in my years of...".
- No personal stories. No "this happened to my aunt", no "I almost fell for this once". The narrator has not lived any of it.

## Never invent
- No fact, number, date, name, place or quote that is not in the source.
- No dialogue presented as real. Mock-up texts and calls are fine only on screen and only under a REENACTMENT label, and they must match what the record says happened (Caught #1: the arrest after a car accident, the bail money).
- No feelings for real people that the record doesn't state. Not "terrified", not "desperate", not "heartbroken".
- When the record skips a step, the script skips it too, or says "here's how this kind of scam usually goes" so viewers know that part is general.

## How the narrator sounds
- Calm, serious, measured. Not hyped, not a movie trailer, not fear-mongering.
- Present tense for the story ("It starts with a phone call"), short sentences, plain words, contractions.
- Respect for victims, always. Never blame them, never make a joke at their expense.
- The tension comes from the order of events and the details, never from adjectives.

## People in the case
- Victims are described the way the record describes them ("seniors", "Victim 1", initials). Never look them up.
- Defendants are described by role, age and city by default. Use a name only if the owner decides to for that episode.
- Charged is not convicted. "Accused" and "prosecutors say" until a guilty plea or a verdict.
- Ages: say whose age and when. The release gives the age at the time of the release, so put "38 (at sentencing)" on screen, and don't attach that age to events years earlier.

## What the channel promotes
- Following for the next numbered episode.
- Reporting scams at reportfraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov.
- Affiliate products only after the owner approves each one, always disclosed out loud or on screen.
