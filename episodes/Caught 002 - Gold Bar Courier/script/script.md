# Caught #2: The Gold Bar Courier

**Takeaway:** No real company or agency will ever ask you to buy gold and hand it to a courier.

**Length:** 254 spoken words, voice 94.0 s, video about 95 s.

**Voice:** eleven_v4, stability 0.4, one storyteller emotion tag per beat (in `voice.json`), no hushed or whisper tags. Every take passed the whisper check (`tools/voice/qc.py`), plus the stutter and pace checks.

## Voiceover
1. In March twenty twenty-four, police in Illinois arrest a man carrying sixteen bars of gold, worth more than a million dollars. Days earlier, that gold belonged to scam victims.
2. It starts on a home computer. Malware puts a phone number on the screen. The victim calls the number.
3. The people on the line pose as Microsoft employees, bank officials, government employees and law enforcement. They say your identity has been compromised, and your money isn't safe in the bank.
4. So the victims, ages sixty-one to eighty, cash out bank and retirement accounts to buy gold. When it arrives, they're told to hand it over for safekeeping. Some are told to address the box to the Department of Justice.
5. One of the people collecting is a courier from Illinois, carrying gold and cash across state lines.
6. On March twenty-fifth and twenty-sixth, he picks up sixteen one-kilogram gold bars from victims in Littleton, Colorado, and Kansas City, Missouri.
7. The next day, the run ends. A sheriff's deputy and a state highway patrol sergeant arrest him in Illinois, gold bars still with him.
8. In May twenty twenty-six, a federal judge in Kansas City sentences him to fifteen and a half years in prison, without parole. Twelve identified victims lost more than six million dollars.
9. No real company or agency will ever ask you to buy gold and hand it to a courier. If a number pops up on your screen, don't call it. Call your bank on the number from your card. Follow for Caught number three.
