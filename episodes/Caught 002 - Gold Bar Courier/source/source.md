# Source: Caught #2

- **Document:** "Illinois Man Sentenced for Elder Fraud Conspiracy Involving Gold Bars", press release
- **Office:** U.S. Attorney's Office, Western District of Missouri
- **Date:** Thursday, May 21, 2026 (sentenced the same day, federal court in Kansas City)
- **URL:** https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmo/pr/illinois-man-sentenced-elder-fraud-conspiracy-involving-gold-bars
- **Status:** federal government work, public domain (17 U.S.C. 105).

## Facts used, with the release's wording
| Fact | Release wording |
| --- | --- |
| How it started | "put malware on people's computers with a phone number to call" |
| Who the callers posed as | "falsely represented themselves as 'Microsoft' employees, banking officials, government employees, and law enforcement officers" |
| What victims were told | "their identities had been compromised, and that their money was not safe in banks" |
| Victims' ages | "ranged in age from 61 to 80 years old" |
| What victims did | "liquidate bank and retirement accounts and buy gold bullion" |
| Safekeeping | "conspirators directed the victims to provide the gold for 'safekeeping' to a co-conspirator" |
| Department of Justice address | victims were sometimes instructed to "address it to the 'Department of Justice' in the name of a specific person" |
| The courier | "An Illinois man", 38 on the sentencing date; picked up gold bullion and cash from victims throughout the country; transported it across state lines |
| The run | On March 25 and 26, 2024, 16 one-kilogram gold bars, worth more than $1 million, from victims in Littleton, Colorado, and Kansas City, Missouri. The release doesn't say which city goes with which date. |
| The arrest | March 27, 2024, "arrested in possession of the gold bars" by a Morgan County, Illinois deputy sheriff and an Illinois State Highway Patrol sergeant |
| Sentence | 15½ years in prison without parole; restitution $4,754,000; forfeiture of the gold bars plus a money judgment |
| Total harm | "Twelve victims identified to date lost over $6 million in the scheme" |

The release does not state the charge, a plea or a verdict, and the script claims none.

## Safety advice check
- FBI IC3 PSA240129: the U.S. government and legitimate businesses will never ask you to buy gold or other precious metals. https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240129
- FTC, "Real government agents aren't asking you to buy and deliver gold bars": don't give bars to a courier. https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/07/real-government-agents-arent-asking-you-buy-deliver-gold-bars
- FTC, "New tech support scammers want your life savings": never call a number on a security pop-up; contact your bank on a number you know is real. https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/03/new-tech-support-scammers-want-your-life-savings
